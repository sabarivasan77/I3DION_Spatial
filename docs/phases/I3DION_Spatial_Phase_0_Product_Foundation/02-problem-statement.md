# I3DION Spatial --- Problem Statement

**Document ID:** PS-PRB-001\
**Version:** 0.2\
**Phase:** Phase 0\
**Status:** Draft for validation

## 1. Core Problem

Industrial manufacturers and sales teams need to communicate complex
physical products clearly, but traditional sales materials often provide
limited spatial understanding.

Customers may need to understand: - What the product looks like - How
large it is - How it fits in a physical environment - What components or
features matter - How the product operates or moves - Whether they need
further technical or commercial discussion

## 2. Current Workflow

A typical workflow may look like:

**Product → Brochure/PDF → Sales Explanation → Images/Video → Physical
Demo/Site Visit → Customer Questions → Follow-up**

This can create repeated explanation, additional coordination, and
uncertainty when spatial context is important.

## 3. User-Level Problem

The customer cannot always form a confident mental model of a complex
industrial product from static material alone.

## 4. Business-Level Problem

Sales and product teams need a more interactive way to demonstrate
products while retaining control over product information, assets,
catalogs, and sales calls-to-action.

## 5. Problem Statement

> Industrial product teams lack an efficient, governed digital workflow
> for turning product information into interactive 3D/AR experiences
> that help customers understand products and move toward a sales
> inquiry.

## 6. Why It Matters

The problem is potentially important when: - Products are physically
large or complex. - A physical demo is expensive or inconvenient. -
Customers need spatial context. - Sales teams repeatedly explain the
same product. - Product information is fragmented across documents and
media. - Customer engagement is difficult to measure.

## 7. Problem Evidence vs Hypothesis

### Existing baseline

The product PRD explicitly frames I3DION Spatial around industrial
product visualization, AR, catalog management, product information,
sales enablement, and lead generation.

### Hypotheses to validate

-   How frequently customers experience spatial uncertainty.
-   Which part of the current workflow is most painful.
-   Whether AR is the preferred solution.
-   Whether QR access is useful in real sales contexts.
-   Whether measurable engagement improves sales outcomes.
-   Whether companies will pay.

## 8. Problem Severity Framework

For each discovered problem, score: - Frequency: 1--5 - Business impact:
1--5 - User frustration: 1--5 - Current workaround cost: 1--5 -
Strategic fit: 1--5

A problem should become a priority candidate only after evidence is
collected.

## 9. Research Questions

1.  How do sales engineers currently demonstrate large/complex products?
2.  What information is hardest for customers to understand?
3.  When is a physical demonstration required?
4.  What tools are already used?
5.  What causes customers to delay a decision?
6.  What evidence would convince a company to adopt a new workflow?
7.  Who owns the budget?
8.  What integrations/security requirements exist?

## 10. Problem Success Criterion

Phase 1 should produce evidence that confirms, modifies, or rejects this
problem statement before the product scope is frozen.
